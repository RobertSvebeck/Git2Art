About Git2Art

Git2Art turns GitHub repositories into artworks and hangs them in a museum. Every picture is drawn from the real structure of the code, so no two repositories look alike, and you can see why.

The Artists

The real artists here are the people who write code and share it with the world. A public repository is a gift: years of thought, care and craft, given away so that anyone can read it, learn from it and build on it. That deserves an audience.

So who is the artist when an AI writes much of the code? Our answer: no single person, and that was always true. A repository is a joint creation of many: its owner, every contributor, the maintainers of the libraries underneath, the people who reported bugs and reviewed changes, and now the AI models they work with. The final result comes from collaboration, and the museum celebrates all of it.

That is why every work carries the name and portrait of the account behind it and how many people contributed. Every artist has a room of their own in the museum, and in the 3D museum you can teleport there from any painting. If a picture speaks to you, star the code. In open source, a star is the applause.

How It Works

Git2Art reads a repository and paints what it finds:

  • Folders - become districts, plants, washes, spirals or bands of thread, depending on the style
  • Files - become buildings, leaves, brushstrokes or beads, sized by how big they are
  • Languages - every language has its own colour, so a Python project and a JavaScript project look different at a glance
  • History - when files appeared and how often they changed decide what flowers, glows, flares or splatters
  • Stability - everything is placed by the file's path, so a small change to the code is a small change to the picture

Key Features

🎨 Eight Art Styles

From gestural painting and watercolour to an 8-bit city, a Mondrian, a botanical garden and a woven tapestry.

🏛️ The Museum

Walk the collection room by room with the arrow keys: new acquisitions, a gallery for every style, the Sculpture Court and the Light Installations. Works on any device.

🌐 The 3D Museum

An endless museum you walk through in your browser: spotlit canvases, repositories as glowing sculptures that replay their history, and dark rooms where a project's commits rise around you as light.

⭐ Celebrating the Artists

Every work names the people behind it, links to their GitHub profile and invites you to star the code. Each artist has a room of their own, one click or one teleport away.

🖼️ Hang Your Own

Paste a public repository on the home page, or click an invitation on the museum wall, and your code is painted and hung within moments.

📐 Adaptive Canvas

The canvas shape follows the project: portrait for mobile apps, landscape for web and documentation-heavy projects, square for the rest.

🔄 Grows With Your Code

Small changes make subtle differences; big refactors transform the picture. Generate again later and see how your project has changed.

The Museum

Every artwork in the collection hangs in the museum, which you can visit in two ways:

  • Museum - room by room on any device: walk along each wall with the arrow keys, press Enter to look closer, M for the floor plan
  • 3D Museum - an endless, walkable building with a door in every wall. North and west lie the Painting Galleries, south the Sculpture Court, east the Light Installations
  • Sculpture Court - each repository as a sculpture: files are glowing beads, folders are bronze branches, and it grows again commit by commit while you stand in the room
  • Light Installations - a project's whole history as a spiral of lights you walk through, with a pulse travelling from the first commit to today
  • The Artists - a room for every account whose code hangs in the museum, with their portrait, their repositories and a star button for each. In 3D, open any painting and teleport straight to its artist's room

Art Styles

Eight styles, each a different way of looking at the same code:

🎨 Expressionist - Gestural bristle-brush painting in the spirit of De Kooning and Kline: a sweeping stroke for each folder, black calligraphy along the folder tree, splatter and drips from the most-edited files (default)
🌸 Impressionist - Thousands of broken brushstrokes swirling around each folder like eddies in a pond, in your languages' colours, with the busiest files catching the light
💧 Watercolour - Real layered glazes on cold-press paper: folders are broad washes, files smaller glazes in their language colour, tied together by an ink sketch of the folder tree
🏙️ Pixel City - Your repository as an isometric 8-bit city: folders become districts, files become buildings (taller for bigger files, coloured by language), documentation becomes parks, and busy projects are painted by day while quiet ones glow at night
🟥 De Stijl - A Mondrian-style composition that is also an exact map of your code: the folder tree divides the canvas by size, line weight follows folder depth, and red, blue and yellow mark your main languages where the code is heaviest or most edited
🌿 Garden - A botanical plate: each folder grows as a plant with its subfolders as branches, files as leaves, the busiest files as flowers in their language colour, documentation as daisies and data files as berries; old code is woody, new code green, and every contributor is a butterfly
🧶 Tapestry - Your project's history woven as cloth: a band of warp threads for every folder, weft rows from the first commit at the top to today at the bottom, and colour wherever that folder was growing
✨ Psychedelic - An op-art poster: every folder is the eye of a spiral, the spirals interfere into neon bands in your languages' colours, the largest folders bloom into mandalas and the busiest files glow as concentric eyes

Language Colours

Each language keeps the same colour in every style, so you learn to read the pictures: Python is blue, JavaScript yellow, TypeScript light blue, HTML orange, CSS violet, Go cyan, Rust copper, Java amber, Ruby red, C# green, Swift coral, and documentation a quiet cream.

Art Inspiration

Each style borrows from a tradition:

  • Abstract Expressionism - the gestures of De Kooning and Franz Kline
  • Impressionism and Post-Impressionism - broken colour, and the swirling strokes of Van Gogh
  • De Stijl - Mondrian's grids of black lines and primary colour
  • Op Art and 1960s posters - interference patterns and neon bands
  • Botanical illustration, weaving and pixel art - for the Garden, the Tapestry and Pixel City

Technical Stack

Backend: Python, Flask
Database: MariaDB
Art Generation: Python with Pillow and NumPy, drawn from each repository's folders, files and history
3D Museum: three.js with WebGL
Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript
